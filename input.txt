Commodities 


	
	Grain
	When it comes to Russian grain prices they begun to drop on the Russian domestic market due to the ongoing attacks on shipping in the sea of Azov and the black Sea. By July 26th year over year wheat prices were down 8,6-8,9% domestically and for export from Novorossyisk on July 24th they were down 9,6% to 15 100 rubles per ton. For barley the prices for export were down even more by 12,2% to 13 000 rubles per ton. The Sea of Azov is non operational and it accounts for 35% of Russian wheat exports. THe expected decrease in exports due to the closure was 6,5 million tons for the 2026-2027 season. On top of that transporting by land to the Black Sea ports adds 1400 rubles in costs as such transportation this year is 27,4% more expensive. 
	
	Further more on July 30th Ukraine hit the Taman grain exporting terminal and stopped its operations. By the end of the week the other two grain termianls in Novorossyisk had limited their exports of grain. The combined output of these 3 facilities is estimated at 20 million tons a year and Russia's exports are around 50 million tons a year when it comes to grain.

	Also impacted by the closure of the Azov sea is sunflower oil and its export. In July month over month exports are down 53.9% and year over year by 14% with a further decrease of 40% expected in August. Prices for it domestically are also down in July by 2,1% for the raw sunflower as export options dwindle. EFKO which is one Russia's largest plant based oil and fats producers also saw turmoil as its executives were arrested in links to the previously reported drone manufacturer arrests in Russia. As they were also in vested in the transportation of the future scheme to attempt to set up large scale drone production for the froent.
	
	
	
	Coal
	When it comes to coking cola Russian prices from the far east ports in  fell by 7,5% over the 30 days between June 17th and July 17th to reach 148 USD per ton under FOB conditions. Pulverized coal fuel prices fell by 2,7% to 144 USD per ton. This is mainly due to the after effects of the large mining incident in China no longer influencing the market. While in June Chinese coal mining dropped by 10% year over by the H1 2026 result was 1% higher than in 2025. Also important is that Chinese steel inventories rose by 21% in June to 9,4 million tons. This coupled with the rain and the weak Chinese real estate sector suggests prices for Russian metallurgical coal for export may drop 10-20% especially with the recovery of Australian output.
	
	On the opposite side of Russsia 6000 kcal coal in the week of July 17th rose in price by 3,6% to 107 USD under CIF conditions. Turkish buyers want to buy Russian coal but Russian producers have sold off their lots in the southern ports. Future deliveries are under question form that basin as Ukraine and Russia have intensified the attacks on shipping in the Black Sea and a coal carrying ship was hit on July 22nd Thus Turkish buyers are looking for alternative routes like coal from the Baltic or through rail through Azerbaijan. Over the next 3 months Turkish buyers may need an extra 0,8-1,5 million tons of Russian coal they can't buy. Due to issues with freight in the Black Sea coal is now being redirected to the Baltic. The problem with that for Russian exporters is the lower turnover rate of ships and that due to the duration of the journey profitability is down 15-30 USD per ton for such deliveries.
